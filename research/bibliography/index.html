BIBLIOGRAPHY

1

“Mapping the Future: Extrapolation in Utopian/Dystopian and Science Fiction”

Journal Article

http://www.jstor.org/stable/20718893

Hardesty, William H. “Mapping the Future: Extrapolation in Utopian/Dystopian and Science Fiction.” Utopian Studies, no. 1, 1987, pp. 160–72. JSTOR, http://www.jstor.org/stable/20718893. Accessed 13 May 2025.

This article explores various case studies in order to investigate the relationship between utopia and science fiction in literature. The main point that I would like to focus on is the idea that it is often the case that such stories are unrelated to the real or "empirical" world; that is, events that unfold do not extrapolate on our reality, but ratheron imagined ones. Thus, such stories are different than the speculation to be done in preparation of and through this Capstone project which should extrapolate on the current state of things.

2

“Grounded Utopia"

Journal Article

https://doi.org/10.5325/utopianstudies.32.3.0552

Davis, Laurence. “Grounded Utopia.” Utopian Studies, vol. 32, no. 3, 2021, pp. 552–81. JSTOR, https://doi.org/10.5325/utopianstudies.32.3.0552. Accessed 13 May 2025.

The key distinction explored by this article is that between aloof and grounded utopias, where (roughly) the former is purely imaginative and not based in history whereas the latter takes history into account when imagining possible futures. Grounded utopias acknowledge history as constantly evolving into and becoming the future. Such specualtion is therefore, arguably, more useful as it explores desireable outcomes that we can reach starting from this point and moving forward.

3

“Utopia/Dystopia"

Journal Article

https://doi.org/10.1086/661960

Greene, Vivien. “Utopia/Dystopia.” American Art, vol. 25, no. 2, 2011, pp. 2–7. JSTOR, https://doi.org/10.1086/661960. Accessed 13 May 2025.

Granted, this article mainly explores attempts to approach utopia through architecture, but I think there is some interesting stuff to glean here. William Morris' name has appeared in a few readings on utopia due to his role in propogating the Arts and Crafts movement which sought to beautify homes, enriching society and subsequently improving lives. This is interesting at face value, because it embodies the creation of a whole (utopian society) through the creation of each consituent part (nurtured members of society). On a larger scope, this begs the question: where does utopia come from? Is it indeed domestic architecture? Or, more probably, does it come from the combination of many perfect parts? I think utopia could not be without compromise of course as it would likely look different for everyone and thus be unattainable, but this sort of exploration is important moving forward in this project.
